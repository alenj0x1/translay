Translay recognizes videos in English, Portuguese, Italian, Japanese, Korean and Spanish and translates and dubs them into English, Spanish or Portuguese: you choose the language once and it applies to the interface, the translation and the voice. Any video can also be only transcribed, in its own language.

## The registry

`vt/langs.py` holds everything that changes from one language to another, as data; the rest of the code doesn't name languages.

| Field | What it is | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|
| `code` | ISO 639-1: Whisper's, the API's and the saved data's. | `pt` |
| `name` | Name in Spanish (the interface's other names are in `NAMES`, one per interface language). | `portugués` |
| `iso3` | ISO 639-2: FFmpeg track tags and the target tag of multilingual Opus-MT (`>>spa<<`). | `por` |
| `nllb` | NLLB-200 token, script included. | `por_Latn` |
| `tts` | Name the voice engines expect. | `Portuguese` |

`SOURCES` are the languages that can be recognized and chosen by hand (`en`, `pt`, `it`, `ja`, `ko`, `es`); `TARGETS` are the ones it translates and dubs into, and the interface can be shown in (`es`, `en`, `pt`). Italian, Japanese and Korean are only sources: they are translated and dubbed, but they aren't targets or interface languages. `target()` is the one chosen now. A video is translated when its language isn't the target, is in the registry and the person didn't choose to only transcribe it.

## One language for everything

**Ajustes › Uso › Idioma** (Settings › Use › Language, `language`) is a single choice for the interface, the translation and the voice, the agent and the summaries. By default it is the system's language (`langs.system()`, which on Windows reads the language of Windows; if it isn't one of the three, English); installations from before there were languages stay in Spanish. Changing it doesn't reload the page: the interface, the open video and the agent switch on the spot.

**Traducir a** (Translate to, `target_lang`) separates the language of translation and dubbing from the app's: by default it is the app's language, but you can read the interface in one language and watch the videos dubbed into another.

## Automatic detection

A new video (imported link, local file or screen recording) has no language: Whisper detects it from the first seconds of speech, before recognizing anything (`WhisperASR.detect`).

- **Queue.** It takes ~15 s of speech found by the energy VAD from three points of the file (the start, a third and two thirds; `speech_samples`) and adds up each language's probability (`vote_language`), so a musical intro, a waiting room or someone greeting in another language doesn't mislead it.
- **Live.** `StreamingTranscriber` waits for 3 s of speech (only the frames with speech) and accepts the language if its probability is 0.7 or more; otherwise it waits for more speech, up to 15 s, and then decides. Below 0.5 it never decides: with noise, music or almost silence Whisper tends to say English, and a Spanish video would then be recognized as English, which Whisper does by translating it (a Zoom class came out «translated» into English and back into Spanish). That stretch isn't marked as covered: the queue processes it with the whole file. The language arrives before the first sentence (`lang` message).
- **Confirmation.** A language decided live (`detected: {by: "vivo"}` in the transcript) is checked by the queue with the whole file before processing it. If it was another one, what was recognized is deleted and redone in the right language (`Transcript.redetect`, `lang` message with `reset`); if the person chose the language, it isn't checked.

A detected language that Translay can't translate yet is only transcribed, in that language. A video from before there were languages, without `lang`, stays in English. A `.en` model passed with `--model` doesn't detect: everything is English to it.

Measured with `tools/lab/langs_asr.py` on FLEURS clips in English, Portuguese and Spanish: 100 % correct with large-v3-turbo, also with chatter in other languages at 5 dB and pink noise at 5 dB, in about 250 ms per detection on an RTX 4060. Whisper `small` got 97.5–100 % with chatter. Italian, on clean FLEURS clips: 100 % with large-v3-turbo (330 ms, confidence 0.999) and with `small`. Japanese and Korean, on clean FLEURS clips: 100 % with large-v3-turbo (219 ms, confidence 0.999) and with `small` (59 ms, 0.991 and 0.974). With chatter in the other five languages at 5 dB or pink noise at 5 dB (60 clips per language), large-v3-turbo `int8_float16` still detects 100 % of Japanese and Korean, and `small` too (confidence 0.93–0.98); the character error rate goes to 6.3 % in Japanese and 6.0 % in Korean with chatter (20.6 % and 13.2 % with `small`).

## Translate or only transcribe

Each video has a mode, independent of its language (`mode` in its [transcript](../data/#per-video-transcript)):

- `translate`: translated and dubbed into the target language (`target()`).
- `transcribe`: only transcribed, in its own language and without voice.

If the person didn't choose a mode, a video detected in the language of the **Solo transcribir videos en** (Only transcribe videos in) preference (`transcribe_lang`, Spanish by default) is only transcribed. Changing the mode deletes nothing: the recognized text works for both and translations are kept, so going back to translating only fills in what's missing. Changing the language does delete what was recognized and processes the video again. In the interface: [Add videos › Language](../../guide/import/#language-and-transcribe-only).

## A translation per language

Each video keeps its translation and its voice in every language it has been watched in. The ones for the active language are in each sentence's fields (`translation`, `dub`, `tr_model`…); the ones for other languages go in `seg["alt"][language]`, and the transcript's `target` field says which language the active ones are in (see [Data on disk](../data/#per-video-transcript)). The dubbed audio is saved as `<sentence>.<language>.wav`.

When you choose another language, what was already done for it comes back and what is missing is translated and dubbed when you open the video. Nothing is deleted, so going back to the first language is instant. `alt` doesn't travel to the browser, which only sees the active language.

## A translator per language pair

`Translators` (`vt/translate.py`) creates the translator for each source language the first time it is needed, according to the target, `ROUTES` and the profile's tier. Measured with `tools/lab/langs_mt.py` on FLEURS (the same sentences in each language, chrF; RTX 4060), these are the routes:

| Pair | Translator (`big` and `base`) | chrF | Per sentence | Against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English → Spanish | Opus-MT tc-big en-es (`opus-big`; `opus-base` in `base`) | 56.7 | | NLLB 55.1, MADLAD 56.7 |
| Portuguese → Spanish | Opus-MT itc-itc (`opus-itc`) | 54.3 | 137 ms | MADLAD 54.5 at 1458 ms, NLLB 53.2 |
| Spanish → Portuguese | Opus-MT itc-itc | 56.1 | 151 ms | MADLAD 57.2 at 1352 ms, NLLB 54.2 |
| English → Portuguese | Opus-MT tc-big en-pt (`opus-big-en-pt`) | 71.6 | 117 ms | MADLAD 70.5, NLLB 68.2 |
| Spanish → English | Opus-MT tc-bible roa-eng (`opus-roa-en`) | 59.9 | 218 ms | MADLAD 60.8, NLLB 59.8 (61.2 with the text rules) |
| Portuguese → English | Opus-MT tc-bible roa-eng | 72.4 | 127 ms | MADLAD 72.2, NLLB 72.2 |
| Italian → Spanish | Opus-MT itc-itc | 52.4 | 208 ms | MADLAD 53.4 at 2039 ms |
| Italian → Portuguese | Opus-MT itc-itc | 57.5 | 195 ms | |
| Italian → English | Opus-MT tc-bible roa-eng | 61.6 | 164 ms | |
| Japanese → Spanish | MADLAD-400 3B (`madlad-3b`) | 46.5 | 1160 ms | Opus-MT tc-bible mul-spa 38.5 at 119 ms, ja-es 35.0 |
| Japanese → Portuguese | MADLAD-400 3B | 51.3 | 1155 ms | Opus-MT tc-bible mul-spa 42.2 |
| Japanese → English | MADLAD-400 3B | 57.1 | 1087 ms | Opus-MT ja-en 47.5 at 82 ms, tc-bible mul-spa 45.7 |
| Korean → Spanish | MADLAD-400 3B | 47.1 | 1050 ms | (no Opus-MT translates it: see below) |
| Korean → Portuguese | MADLAD-400 3B | 52.4 | 1192 ms | |
| Korean → English | MADLAD-400 3B | 57.9 | 990 ms | Opus-MT tc-big ko-en 55.7 at 96 ms |

Any other pair, or a model that doesn't support the pair, goes to `FALLBACK`: MADLAD-400 3B. It replaced NLLB-200, whose license is CC-BY-NC, with Apache-2.0, and it ties or beats it on the pairs measured.

- **One on the GPU.** Switching language moves the previous translator to RAM (about 1 s to come back), so the memory measured for each [profile](../../guide/performance/) doesn't grow with languages.
- **Download outside the GPU thread.** A translator missing on disk is downloaded without blocking recognition or voice of another video. Live, sentences stay untranslated and are filled in when it's ready.
- **Japanese and Korean on demand.** Their translator, MADLAD-400 3B (about 3 GB), isn't downloaded with the profile (`ON_DEMAND` in `vt/langs.py`, `modelset.on_demand`) but with the first Japanese or Korean video; once there, changing profile doesn't list it as unused.
- **`--translator`** forces one model for every language it supports; the others keep their route.

itc-itc and roa-eng are multilingual models for Romance languages: they need the target tag (`>>spa<<`, `>>por<<`, from `iso3`) at the start of each sentence.

### Preparing the Opus-MT models

itc-itc and roa-eng aren't published in CTranslate2 format, so `vt/opusmt.py` prepares them from the official OPUS-MT release (Helsinki-NLP, Tatoeba Challenge) the first time a pair needs them. roa-eng (tc-bible, 2024, Apache-2.0) is published like itc-itc and is converted as if it were one:

<Steps>

1. Downloads the release at a pinned URL (itc-itc 789 MB, roa-eng 866 MB) and checks its SHA-256; if it doesn't match, it is discarded.
2. Converts it with CTranslate2's converter, which only needs numpy, not torch (about 3 s), to `float16`.
3. Leaves it in `translay/opus-<name>` next to the Hugging Face cache (or in `TRANSLAY_MODELS`), with the original `LICENSE` and `README`, and deletes the intermediate files. The final folder only appears when it is complete.

</Steps>

itc-itc's license is CC-BY-4.0.

Portuguese → Spanish, test, 347 sentences, in detail:

| Translator | chrF | Per sentence | Sentences/s (batches) | VRAM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Opus-MT itc-itc** | 54.3 | 137 ms | 69 | ~0.7 GB |
| NLLB-200 1.3B | 53.2 (54.3 with the text rules) | 953 ms | 11.7 | ~2 GB |
| MADLAD-400 3B | 54.5 | 1458 ms | 6.3 | ~3.5 GB |

itc-itc is as good as MADLAD and ten times faster. As a reference, English → Spanish: opus-big 56.7, opus-base 55.2, NLLB 55.1, MADLAD 56.7.

| Model | English | Portuguese | Spanish |

| large-v3-turbo `int8_float16` | 4.9 % | 4.2 % | 3.3 % |
| small `int8` | 6.3 % | 7.7 % | 6.3 % |
| large-v3-turbo `int8`, chatter at 5 dB | 17.0 % | 11.1 % | 7.9 % |

Italian (FLEURS `it_it`, same lab, 120 clean clips): large-v3-turbo `float16` 3.5 %, small `int8_float16` 9.2 %.

Japanese has no spaces, so its word error rate counts whole sentences (83 % with any model): the lab also reports the character error rate (CER, without spaces or punctuation, digits and letters in their half-width form). FLEURS `ja_jp` and `ko_kr`, 120 clean clips:

| Model | Japanese CER | Korean WER | Korean CER |
|---|---|---|---|
| large-v3-turbo `float16` | 3.6 % | 13.3 % | 4.7 % |
| small `int8_float16` | 12.2 % | 21.4 % | 7.3 % |

Korean words carry their particles («서버를»), so one wrong syllable is a whole wrong word: its CER is the closer measure to what you read.

Japanese and Korean, FLEURS test (320 and 269 sentences) into the three targets: MADLAD-400 3B wins every pair, by 8 to 10 points over every Opus-MT measured (Japanese → Spanish 46.5 against 38.5 for the multilingual tc-bible mul-deu_eng_fra_por_spa and 35.0 for ja-es), except Korean → English, where tc-big ko-en gets 55.7 against 57.9 and is ten times faster (96 ms against 990 ms). Two points is beyond the one point where the fast model is preferred, and Korean already needs MADLAD for Spanish and Portuguese, so it uses MADLAD too. mul-deu_eng_fra_por_spa doesn't translate Korean: it returns the Korean text, garbled (chrF below 1); opus-mt-ko-es (2020) isn't downloadable from OPUS any more. MADLAD takes ~1.1 s per sentence (7–10 sentences/s in batches, ~3.5 GB of VRAM), like for Italian.

Italian → Spanish, test, 344 sentences: Opus-MT itc-itc 52.4 at 208 ms per sentence and 36 sentences/s in batches (~0.7 GB), MADLAD-400 3B 53.4 at 2039 ms and 4.1 sentences/s (~3.5 GB). MADLAD is one point better and ten times slower, so Italian uses the itc-itc that Portuguese already downloads; into Portuguese (345 sentences) itc-itc gets 57.5, and roa-eng into English (346) 61.6. As a reference, the Opus-MT leaderboard gives these models 52.2, 58.2 and 62.4 on FLORES-200 devtest.

## Text rules

Rules that depend on the language are keyed by its code; a language without its own only gets what doesn't depend on the language (punctuation and pauses).

- **Before translating** (`vt/textproc.py`): abbreviations that don't end a sentence ("Mr.", "Sra.", "Sig.", "Dott.") and idioms rewritten to their meaning. In Portuguese, idioms that the translator would render literally ("pisou na bola" → "errou", "custou os olhos da cara" → "custou muito caro", "moleza" → "muito fácil", "fica ligado" → "fique atento", "fala, galera" → "olá, pessoal") and colloquial forms that Whisper writes as they are spoken ("pra" → "para", "tá" → "está", "bora" → "vamos", "tô", "cê", "né"). In Italian, "costa un occhio della testa" → "costa moltissimo" (itc-itc rendered it «un ojo de la cabeza»). In Korean, the English interjections written in Hangul go to MADLAD in their meaning, in English: «파이팅»/«화이팅» → «Let's go» and «짠»/«짜잔» → «Ta-da» (it rendered them «¡Lucha!» and «¡Azúcar!», and Korean rewrites like «힘내자» as «¡Esfuérzate!»); «짠» only as an interjection, since it also means salty. Japanese has no spaces or capitals: it is split after 。！？ (and the closing quote 」 that follows), not when a quotation continues («「すごい！」と言った»); Korean has no capitals, so a period and a space are enough. The translation keeps its final period when the original ends in 。, a fragment ending in 、 keeps its comma, and capitals aren't copied from a script that has none.
- **Live recognition** (`vt/streaming.py`): words a sentence rarely ends with, words written in lowercase after a comma, and the phrases Whisper makes up on silence in each language ("Legendas pela comunidade Amara.org", "Obrigado por assistir", "Sottotitoli creati dalla comunità Amara.org", "Sottotitoli a cura di QTSS", "Subtítulos realizados por la comunidad de Amara.org", "Gracias por ver el video"). Before, there were only English rules; Spanish videos also benefit. Whisper splits Japanese into pieces of a few characters, so a Japanese sentence is joined without spaces, a pause closes it with 。 instead of «.», and it needs 5 characters (instead of 3 words) to count as a sentence.

The Japanese and Korean phrases come from `tools/lab/langs_silence.py`, which feeds 66 clips without voice (silence, white, pink and brown noise, mains hum, chords) with the language given: large-v3-turbo wrote «ご視聴ありがとうございました» on 57 of them and «ありがとうございました» on 9; in Korean «다음 영상에서 만나요» on 44, «감사합니다» on 14, «아멘», «시청해주셔서 감사합니다» and a subtitle credit («한글자막 by 한효정»). `small` added «最後までご視聴ありがとうございました», «구독과 좋아요 부탁드려요» and «고맙습니다». Like the others, they are dropped only when they are the whole sentence and their confidence is below 0.9. With the same confidence rule, a Korean sentence that starts as a subtitle credit («한글자막 제공», «자막 by…», with any name after it) is dropped too, and so is, in any language, one that ends with the same two to four words repeated three times: over the music of a TWICE video Whisper wrote «한글자막 제공 및 자막 제공 및 자막 제공 및».

## Language of the interface

The interface text is written in Spanish in the code and looked up in a catalog for the app's language. The catalogs are `web/i18n/en.json` and `pt.json` (the key is the Spanish text); Spanish needs none. In the browser, `t("Agregar")` (`web/js/i18n.js`) returns the catalog's text, with `{variables}` where there are values (`t("Quedan {n} videos", { n: 3 })`), and what is missing stays in Spanish. On the server, `i18n.t()` (`vt/i18n.py`) does the same for what it writes: the preferences' labels and descriptions, the agent's cards, notices. The server delivers the catalog at `/i18n.js` before the interface scripts, and dates and numbers use the language's format (`es-419`, `en-US`, `pt-BR`).

`tools/i18n.py` collects every text from the code (`t("…")` in `web/js`, the fixed texts and attributes of `index.html`, `i18n.t("…")` and the preferences in `vt/`), compares them with each catalog and lists what is missing or left over; `--fill` adds what is missing, empty, and `--prune` removes what the code no longer uses. `tests/test_i18n.py` requires every catalog to have all the texts with the same `{variables}`.

## The agent's and the summary's language

The prompts of the agent, the summary (`vt/digest.py`), the short description and the fallback summary (`vt/summarize.py`) are written in English with the order to answer in the app's language (`PROMPT` in `vt/langs.py`: Latin American Spanish, English or Brazilian Portuguese), plus a language reminder at the end of the request, after the video.

The reminder matters: with a question in another language, small models follow the language of the question or of the video, not the instruction at the top. `tools/lab/lang_adherence.py` asked in a language different from the one requested: without the reminder the answers came out in the requested language 0 out of 5 times in English; with it, 93–100 % in the four agent models (Qwen3.5 4B, Gemma 4 E2B, Qwen3.5 2B, Qwen3.5 0.8B) and the three languages.

The agent's quick actions (`vt/agentintents.py`) recognize requests in each language with phrase tables (`PHRASES`): you can ask in Spanish, English or Portuguese and it answers in the app's.

The voice engines OmniVoice, Chatterbox and VoxCPM receive the target language, with the name from the registry (`tts`).

## Add a language

<Steps>

1. Add its entry to `LANGS` in `vt/langs.py`, its code to `SOURCES` and, if it will also be a target and an interface language, to `TARGETS`, with its names in `NAMES`, `NATIVE` and `PROMPT`. With that it can be detected, chosen in the interface and in the API, and transcribed.
2. Create its interface catalog, `web/i18n/<code>.json`, add it to `LANGS` in `tools/i18n.py` and run `python tools/i18n.py --fill` to list the texts to translate; `tests/test_i18n.py` fails until it is complete. Add its phrases to `PHRASES` in `vt/agentintents.py`.
3. Measure translators for each pair with `tools/lab/langs_mt.py` and `tools/lab/langs_asr.py` for recognition and detection, and check the agent's language with `tools/lab/lang_adherence.py`.
4. If one wins over MADLAD, add it to `MODELS` and its route to `ROUTES` in `vt/translate.py`; without a route, MADLAD-400 3B translates it. If it isn't published for CTranslate2, add its official release to `PUBLISHED` in `vt/opusmt.py`. If its translator is large and only this language needs it (MADLAD for Japanese and Korean), add the language to `ON_DEMAND`, so it is downloaded with the first video and not with every profile.
5. Optional: its text rules in `ABBR` and `IDIOMS` (`vt/textproc.py`) and in `NO_END`, `LOWER_AFTER_COMMA` and `HALLUCINATIONS` (`vt/streaming.py`), checked against the lab's typical sentences; the hallucinations, measured with `tools/lab/langs_silence.py`. A language written without spaces also goes in `NO_SPACES` (and its period in `PERIOD`), and its sentence splitting in `_SPLIT` (`vt/textproc.py`).
6. Add its cases to `tests/test_langs.py`.

</Steps>
